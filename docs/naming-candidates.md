# App Naming Candidates

## Creative Directions

- **Ironic idioms** (1-3): English phrases with built-in money wink and understatement humor
- **Embedded meaning** (4-5): Real words with financial meaning hidden inside
- **Portmanteau** (6): Multiple meanings compressed into one invented word
- **Bloom compounds** (7-9): "Bloom" as prefix paired with money/value words

## Evaluation Criteria

- Money/savings DNA (not earn, cash, or money explicitly)
- Makes the user feel smart ("I get it" moment)
- Feels like a hub/assistant for topping up household money
- App store safe
- Resonates with target audience (women 45+, US, "Smart Earner" psychographic)
- Aligns with brand values: Simplicity, Reliability, Valuable

## Other Names Explored

These came up during brainstorming and had some merit but didn't make the shortlist:

- **Earnestly** — variant of Earnest, more distinctive but longer
- **Feather** — from "feather your nest," effortless enrichment
- **Harvest** — gathering fruits of labor, contains "vest" (too common)
- **Keeper** — "she's a keeper" + safekeeping
- **Perennial** — comes back every season, reliable (too long)
- **Steep** — extracting value slowly, "steep return" (too short)
- **Marigold** — flower with "gold" embedded, hotel/cottage vibe
- **Fiorino** — Italian gold coin named after a flower (too foreign for US 45+)
- **Nestworth** — nest + net worth portmanteau
- **Bloomwell** — "blooming well" + wellspring
- **Bloomworth** — bloom + worth/net worth
- **Bloomstead** — homestead where things grow
- **Small Fortune** — ironic idiom (liked the wink, not the name)
- **Pennypot** — piggy bank direction (too cozy/small)
- **Goldfinch** — bird with gold in the name (too nature-brand)
- **Windfall** — unexpected bonus (too passive)
- **Encore** — second act (no money DNA)

## Notes

- **Hervest** commits to gendered positioning ("Her") — depends on team vote on audience targeting breadth
- **Vesta** has deepest backstory (goddess of home, autonomy, power) — maps to "Being Seen" audience insight
- **PrettyPenny** tested strongest through iterative feedback rounds
