## Brand Positioning

### Positioning (deciding)

Moving beyond points and gimmicks to become the app people actually trust with their spare time. Small actions, real value, no tricks.

**Options under consideration:**

| Option | Angle |
|--------|-------|
| **"Small Wins. Real Savings."** | Modest + concrete. The piggy bank promise — small effort, tangible accumulation. |
| **"Your Everyday Piggy Bank"** | Warm, familiar, zero scam associations. Nostalgic and store-safe. |
| **"Small Moments. Real Value."** | Her-centric. Reframes spare time as something that produces real worth. |

**Focus:** Transparency and respect for your time. Designed with women 45+ in mind. Welcoming to everyone.

**Voice:** Encouraging, not fluffy. Clear, not cold. Trusted, not trendy.

## Target Audience

**Psychographic: "The Smart Earner"**
Age 40–65, US. Financially practical, not desperate. Manages a household budget. Plays casual games during downtime. Uses PayPal, Rakuten, or Amazon regularly. Discovers apps through friends and reviews — never influencers. Deletes anything scammy within 30 seconds. Routine-driven.

> *"I don't need to get rich. I just want my time to be worth something."*

**The "Being Seen" insight:** This audience feels invisible — undervalued at work, taken for granted at home, overlooked by products built for younger demographics. The brand opportunity is to make her feel recognized, valued, and smart for what she does. Every interaction should reinforce: *someone built this with you in mind.*

### Primary: The Budget-Conscious Mom

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| Archetype | Lisa, 47, suburban Ohio. Part-time work, family of four. |
| Motivation | Covers small recurring costs — subscriptions, school supplies, pharmacy. |
| Apps | Facebook, Amazon, PayPal, Pinterest, Candy Crush. |
| Discovery | A friend texts: "this app actually pays out." |
| Dealbreaker | Feels like it's wasting her time or hiding how earnings work. |

> *"If it's real and it's simple, I'll check it every morning with my coffee."*

### Secondary Personas (condensed)

| Persona | Key trait | Motivation | Dealbreaker |
|---------|-----------|------------|-------------|
| Diane, 62, retired teacher, FL | Cautious optimizer | Mental stimulation + treats | Confusing UI, earning feels pointless |
| Carlos, 58, warehouse supervisor, AZ | Routine earner | Extra cash for hobbies | Anything taking >5 min to learn |
| Tom, 52, IT manager, TX | Silent optimizer | Household budget gains | Gimmicky feel, aggressive notifications |

## Brand Values

Three core values. Every feature, screen, and sentence should pass at least one.

| Value | Meaning | Product test |
|-------|---------|-------------|
| **Simplicity** | One glance tells you what to do and what you'll get. | Can Lisa use this screen without instructions? |
| **Reliability** | It works consistently. Earnings arrive on time. No surprises. | Would Carlos trust this after a week? |
| **Valuable** | Your time produces real, visible returns. No points games. | Does this screen show a dollar amount? |

## Brand Guardrails

What we are NOT — if [APP_NAME] ever feels like any of these, something is wrong.

| We are NOT | What this means | Example violation |
|------------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Confusing** | Every screen has one clear action. No jargon, no hidden mechanics. | A dashboard with 6 different "earn" buttons and no hierarchy. |
| **Spammy** | We notify only when it matters. No countdown timers. No FOMO. | "Don't miss out! Your rewards are waiting — come back before they expire!" |
| **Cold** | We speak like a helpful neighbor, not a corporation. Warm, not robotic. | "Error 403: Request could not be processed." |

**The Lisa test:** Would this make Lisa delete the app after 30 seconds?
**The referral test:** Would this make Lisa text a friend "you have to try this app"?

### Do / Don't Examples

| Context | Do (on-brand) | Don't (off-brand) |

**The pattern:** State what happened, state the value, move on. No hype. No fake urgency. Encouraging, not excitable.

## Illustration Direction

**Style: "Gentle Linework"** — flowing continuous-line illustrations in deep sage, with transparent watercolor washes in the brand palette. Think elegant editorial line art — confident strokes drawn without lifting the pen. Modern continuous-line art meets Matisse.

### Visual Characteristics

- **Stroke:** Confident continuous line, ~2px, deep sage (#4A6F4A), smooth with gentle organic curves
- **Fills:** Transparent watercolor washes at 30–40% opacity in blush (#F2DCD9), lavender (#E2DCF0), sage-light (#D4E6D5) — max 2 at full wash, 3rd allowed as subtle bleed at <20%
- **No outlines on fills** — color bleeds past linework like loose watercolor behind pen
- **No faces on people** — hands and simple silhouettes only (avoids representation bias for diverse 40–65 audience)
- **Rounded terminals** on all strokes, matching Nunito's personality
- **No hard shadows or gradients**

### Energy Levels

Illustrations follow two modes based on context:
- **Calm/grounded** (task moments): mailbox, coffee, survey, receipt scan, empty state — stable compositions, objects at rest
- **Dynamic/bouncy** (money/reward moments): coin drop, wallet cashout, high five, piggy bank, streak — floating elements, tilted angles, motion lines

This maps to the emotional arc: calm task → dynamic reward.

### Spot Illustrations (8–10 assets)

| Illustration | Where it appears |
|-------------|-----------------|
| Piggy bank with a sprout growing from the slot | Home empty state, onboarding |
| Hand dropping a coin into a jar | Task completed |
| Mailbox with an envelope peeking out | New task available |
| Coffee cup with a dollar sign in the steam | Daily check-in |
| Open wallet with a PayPal card visible | Cashout screen |
| Two hands high-fiving | Milestone / streak |
| Magnifying glass over a receipt | Receipt scan task |
| Chat bubble with checkmarks | Survey / poll task |

These are spot illustrations — roughly 120–160px in use, sitting alongside text, never replacing it.

### Icon Set

For 20–30 functional icons (nav, actions, status):
- 24×24px grid, 2px stroke, rounded caps and joins
- Sage (#5E8A60) when active, neutral (#9E968B) when inactive
- Filled variant for selected states — same shape, sage fill at 12% opacity behind stroke
- No emoji in production — custom icons in this line style make the brand feel owned, not rented

### What to Avoid

- **Generic flat illustration** (Undraw, Humaaans) — signals "we didn't invest in brand"
- **3D / Blush renders** — skews young, feels like a gaming app
- **Photographic imagery** — hard to maintain consistency
- **Mascot character** — under consideration per team vote (March 2026). If used, must be rendered in the linework style, not a separate art direction. No fox (competitor overlap with Scrambly). Species TBD.
- **Overly polished vector art** — in 2026, audiences read perfection as AI-generated and lose trust

### Commissioning

Brief a single illustrator (not a team) for all spots + icon set. One hand = one consistent wobble. Budget for 2–3 days. Deliver as SVG for crisp Flutter rendering at any scale.

Full illustrator brief with AI prompts and delivery specs: `docs/plans/2026-03-11-illustration-animation-direction.md`

## Animation Direction

**Celebration animation: "Sage Sparkle"** — a reusable 1.5-second Lottie that works as overlay on any success moment. A soft burst of organic shapes radiating from center, like a dandelion puff catching light.

### Spec Summary

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Canvas | 300×300px, transparent |
| Duration | 1.5s (~45 frames at 30fps) |
| Total shapes | 19 (1 center + 12 petals + 6 shimmer dots) |
| Palette | #5E8A60, #D4E6D5, #F2DCD9, #E2DCF0, #8BAB8D |
| Easing | ease-out-cubic on position/scale, linear on opacity |
| Export | Lottie JSON, <50kb target |

### Usage Matrix

| Trigger | Scale |
|---------|-------|
| Task completed | 0.6× (centered on task card) |
| Daily check-in | 0.5× (behind check icon) |
| Cashout confirmed | 1.0× (centered on balance card) |
| Streak milestone | 0.7× (behind streak counter) |
| First-time actions | 1.2× (centered on screen) |

Scale it, don't redesign it. One animation, one identity.

### Principles

- **Organic, not geometric** — ellipses instead of circles, slight randomness in rotation
- **Brand palette only** — sage, blush, lavender at reduced opacities
- **Gentle physics** — everything decelerates, nothing bounces or snaps
- **Warm, not celebratory** — feels like a sigh of satisfaction, not a slot machine payout
- **Contained** — accents the moment without hijacking it; no screen takeover, no looping

Full animation specs (Sage Sparkle phases, Piggy Bloom cashout sequence, easing curves): `docs/plans/2026-03-11-illustration-animation-direction.md`

## Copy Guidelines

Concrete patterns for anyone writing [APP_NAME] copy.

### Showing Earnings

Always use real dollar amounts: `$0.25`, `$10.00`. Never points, coins, or gems. But raw dollars alone aren't enough — **frame earnings as progress toward goals, never show cents in isolation.**

**Core principle: Never let the user do the math.** Every time she sees a raw cent amount and calculates an hourly rate in her head, you lose. Do the math *for* her — upward, cumulative, framed as progress.

**Goal-based framing (primary system):**

Goals are the structural backbone of how we show earnings. Users set their own savings goals with their own labels. The goal gradient effect (Kivetz et al., 2006) shows people accelerate effort as they approach a target — small reachable goals create the steepest motivation curves.

| Instead of | Say |
|------------|-----|
| "Balance: $12.40" | "Coffee fund: $12.40 / $25" |
| "$47.50 this month" | "Grocery goal: $47.50 / $50 — almost there!" |
| "Cashout: $25.00" | "You hit your $25 goal — ready to cash out?" |
| "You earned $0.40" | "You earned $0.40 — $2.10 to your next goal" |

- **Name the goal, not the balance.** Users choose their own goal label ("vacation fund," "holiday gifts," "rainy day"). This captures the concreteness of expense anchoring while preserving autonomy.
- **Show the shrinking gap.** "$2.60 to go" is more motivating than "$12.40 earned" — the number getting smaller pulls people forward.
- **Celebrate milestones.** "You're halfway to your coffee goal" triggers a burst of motivation right when engagement typically dips.
- **Progressive goals.** "You hit $5 — let's see if you can get to $10 this week." Each completed goal sets up the next.

**Why goals over expense reminders:** Team feedback (March 2026) and behavioral research both point the same direction. Expense anchoring ("$12 — that's half a tank of gas") implicitly sets the reference point at the *expense*, reminding users of financial obligations. For our "financially cautious" audience, this triggers scarcity cues and avoidance (Mullainathan & Shafir, 2013). Goal framing keeps the reference point at zero — everything is gain. It supports autonomy (Deci & Ryan, 2000) and the endowed progress effect (Nunes & Dreze, 2006).

**Cumulative framing — lead with the big number:**

| Feels like pennies | Feels like it matters |
|--------------------|----------------------|
| $0.40 per task | $47 this month |
| 3 min for $0.40 | $564 this year |
| Daily earnings: $1.25 | "Since joining, you've earned $187" |

Show the running lifetime total prominently. The cumulative number only goes up, creating a psychological ratchet of progress.

**Time framing — avoid time-next-to-cents:**
"3 min" next to "$0.40" invites hourly-wage math ($8/hr — insulting). Instead:
- "While your coffee brews" — frames it as dead time recaptured, not labor
- "Quick poll · $0.40" — task name first, reward second, no time
- Show time only when it's a selling point: "30 sec · $0.15" feels like free money

**Social proof — concrete and personal:**
Not "users earned $10M" (too abstract). Instead: "Lisa, people like you earned an average of **$47 last month**." This answers the unspoken question: "Is this actually worth my time?"

**Monthly impact statement — once/month push or modal:**

> **Your March earnings: $52.40**
>
> Your vacation fund is at **$127.80**
>
> Since you joined: **$187.20 earned**

This reframes scattered micro-earnings into a monthly outcome she can point to and say "this is helping." Note: the statement references *her* goal, not an imposed expense category.

**Expense context (optional, user-controlled only):**

Expense equivalences are NOT used as system-imposed copy. However, users may choose goal labels that reference expenses ("grocery fund," "Netflix money"), and that's fine — the key is *she* chose it, not us. If we ever surface expense context:
- Only aspirational/treat categories (coffee, movie night) — never obligations (gas, rent, bills, prescriptions)
- Only when the user has opted into it via their goal label
- Never as the primary framing — always secondary to goal progress

Full playbook with sources: `docs/plans/2026-03-11-earnings-framing-strategy.md`

### Celebrating
- Understated warmth. Acknowledge the milestone, state the value, move on.
- Do: "Nice — you just hit $25 total." Don't: "AMAZING! You're a superstar earner!"
- Streaks: `5-day streak` over `5 days in a row — amazing!`
- **Animation:** Use the "Sage Sparkle" Lottie for celebration moments — warm bloom, not confetti. Single play, contained to the card, no screen takeover. See Animation Direction below.

### Onboarding
- Get to the first earn within 60 seconds. Educate by doing, not by touring.
- Day 1 feel: "Here's how it works" over "You're going to love this."
- Permission requests: explain the benefit. "This helps us find better offers for you" over "Enable notifications."

### Handling Errors & Empty States
- Be specific and helpful. State what happened and what to do next.
- Do: "No tasks available right now. We'll notify you when new ones arrive."
- Don't: "Oops, nothing here yet!" (too cute) or "Error 404" (too cold).

### Saying No
- Always pair a no with a next step.
- Do: "You're not eligible for this one — here are 3 others you can do now."
- Don't: "Not available."

### Session Endings
- State the outcome. Do: "Summary: $1.25 earned today." Don't: "Great job today!"

## App Store Language

Apple and Google are increasingly hostile to "earning app" positioning. This constrains our store listing copy, metadata, screenshots, and in-app language visible during review.

**Avoid in store-facing contexts:**
- "Cash out" / "cashback" / "earn cash"
- "Make money" / "earn money"
- "Get paid"
- "Discovery" (in the offer/reward sense)

**Use instead:**
- Savings framing: "save," "rewards," "your piggy bank"
- Value language: "your time, real value," "small wins"
- Activity-based: "complete tasks," "share your opinion," "scan receipts"

This is one reason the positioning pivot toward piggy bank / savings language matters — it's not just better branding, it's a store approval requirement.
