## Who She Is

Before we show the direction, it's worth grounding in *why* every choice was made the way it was. It starts with understanding who we're actually building for.

### The Financial Reality

Our audience isn't desperate — she's cautious. She feels the squeeze and wants to ease it.

- 65% of women cite finances as their top stressor
- 37% of Americans can't cover a $400 emergency
- 92% of Americans cut back spending in 2025 — including groceries and healthcare

She's not looking for a side hustle. She wants small, reliable ways to stretch the household budget. $50 a month — a tank of gas, a week of groceries, the pharmacy copay that was causing stress.

### The "Being Seen" Insight

This is the emotional foundation of everything we're building.

This audience feels invisible in much of their daily life. Undervalued at work. Taken for granted at home. Overlooked by products built for younger demographics. Very few things are designed *for her*.

That's our opportunity: make her feel recognized, valued, and smart for what she does. Every screen, every word, every interaction should quietly say: *someone built this with you in mind.*

This isn't just nice positioning — it's the reason she stays, and the reason she texts a friend about it.

### The Trust Threshold

She deletes anything scammy within 30 seconds. Literally. Unrealistic claims, aggressive notifications, confusing point systems, countdown timers — any one of these and she's gone. Forever.

Trust isn't a feature we add. It's the baseline everything is built on.

## The Bridge: From Values to Visual

Our alignment decisions already answered most of the design questions. We just had to follow them.

| Because we said… | The direction… |
|---|---|
| **Simplicity** — one glance tells you what to do | Uses generous white space, big typography, one clear action per screen |
| **Reliability** — no surprises | Feels grounded and calm. Real dollar amounts are always the largest element |
| **Valuable** — real returns, not points | Shows earnings anchored to real expenses she recognizes |
| **Encouraging, not formal** | Speaks like a helpful neighbor, not a corporation |
| **Soft & rounded** | Uses warm palette, rounded type, gentle shadows — without being childish |
| **Not spammy** | Celebrates with warmth, not fireworks. No urgency, no FOMO |
| **Not cold** | Brings in hand-drawn illustration and human touch |

None of these choices were arbitrary. Each one traces back to something we decided together.

---

## The Palette

Colors needed to say "safe and real" — not "startup" and not "coupon app."

**Sage green** — trust, calm, growth. Grounding color. Not corporate blue, not fintech teal.

**Blush & lavender** — warmth and softness, without being "pink." Adds personality without tipping into gendered territory.

**Warm cream** — background feels like paper, not a screen. Inviting, not sterile.

The full palette is muted and warm. Nothing shouts. Nothing competes for attention. The only thing that pops is the dollar amount — because that's what matters.

---

## The Typography

We're using **Outfit** — rounded enough to feel friendly, structured enough to make dollar amounts feel real.

- Headlines are large and confident. The app speaks clearly, not quietly.
- Dollar amounts are always the biggest element on screen — because we said *Valuable*.
- Weight and size create hierarchy without needing color tricks or visual noise.

The type does the work so the design doesn't have to be loud.

---

## The Illustration Direction: Gentle Linework

This is where "not cold" and "soft & rounded" come together visually.

**The style:** Continuous-line illustrations in deep sage with transparent watercolor washes behind them. One confident stroke, never lifting the pen. Think modern editorial line art meets Matisse — not generic stock illustration.

**Why this style:**

- Generic flat illustration (Undraw, Humaaans) signals "we didn't invest in this." She notices.
- 3D renders skew young and feel like a gaming app.
- Photography is hard to maintain consistently.
- One illustrator's hand gives us a personality that feels *owned*, not rented.

**Key details:**

- No faces on people — hands and silhouettes only. Avoids representation bias for a diverse 40–65 audience.
- Watercolor washes in blush, lavender, sage at 30–40% opacity. Color bleeds past the linework — loose, not precise.
- Two energy levels that map to the user's emotional arc:
  - **Calm** for task moments — coffee cup, mailbox, receipt. Stable, grounded.
  - **Dynamic** for reward moments — coin drop, high five, piggy bank sprout. Floating elements, gentle motion.

Calm task → dynamic reward. The illustration mirrors what the app does.

---

## The Animation Philosophy: Sage Sparkle

One celebration animation. Scaled to the moment, never redesigned per screen.

**What it feels like:** A soft burst of organic shapes radiating from center — like a dandelion puff catching light. Sage, blush, lavender at reduced opacity. Everything decelerates. Nothing bounces or snaps.

**The principle:** A sigh of satisfaction, not a slot machine payout.

- Warm, not flashy
- Contained to the card — never takes over the screen
- Single play, then gone
- Lisa should smile, not flinch

Because we said *not spammy*, our celebrations are quiet. They acknowledge the moment and move on.

## The Voice: How We Talk

Our tone follows one pattern: **state what happened, state the value, move on.** No hype. No fake urgency. Encouraging, not excitable.

### Side by side

| Moment | Our voice | Not our voice |
|---|---|---|
| First earning | "Nice — you just earned $0.25. It adds up faster than you think." | "Ka-ching! $0.25 earned! Keep going to unlock more!" |
| Balance | "$12.40 earned — that's half a tank of gas." | "Balance: $12.40" |
| Cashout | "Your $10.00 is on its way to PayPal. You should see it within 24 hours." | "Woohoo! $10 cashed out! Treat yourself — you earned it!" |
| Re-engagement | "You have 3 new surveys. Estimated: $1.50." | "Don't miss out! Your rewards are waiting — come back before they expire!" |
| Empty state | "No tasks right now. We'll notify you when new ones arrive." | "Oops, nothing here yet!" |

### The earnings rule

**Never let her do the math.** $0.40 next to "3 minutes" invites hourly-wage calculation ($8/hr — insulting). Instead:

- Anchor to real expenses: "$12.40 earned — that's half a tank of gas"
- Lead with cumulative totals: "$47 this month" not "$0.40 per task"
- Frame time as context, not labor: "While your coffee brews" not "3 minutes"
- Name the goal: "Grocery fund: $12.40 / $50" not just "Balance: $12.40"

The earnings are real. The framing is what makes them *feel* real.
